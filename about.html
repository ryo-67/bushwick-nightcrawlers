The Bushwick Nightcrawlers

what this is

A sound piece scored for the corner of Myrtle and Broadway at night, narrated as Yelp reviews by the rats who live there. Click any pin on the map to read a review and hear it spoken in rat vocalizations, some slowed from ultrasound into human hearing range.

The rats are not the customers. They are under the floorboards, up through the bathroom drainpipes, in the dumpsters, licking residue off discarded napkins. Yelp is the form, not the genre. The genre is ambient documentary.

The piece sits over a continuous score of the intersection itself. You hear the JMZ overhead, the ambient drone of the corner, small punctuations of street activity. The reviews layer in as you click, and stay layered. By the time you’ve heard them all, the corner is louder than when you arrived.

why

A rat couple lives outside my apartment. They used to come out together at night to forage. All anyone says about rats is that they’re dirty, but to me they seemed like a sweet couple. They might be dirty, but they were also pretty good at dirty dancing.

A few weeks ago the sidewalk was taped off. Exterminators had been through the block. I was sadder than I expected to be.

The burrow opened up again a few days later. Same spot. I still see them sometimes.

That’s how this project started. I wanted to know what they were saying.

on the rats

The cast is fixed. When Caffeine Underground closed in March 2026, the rats inherited the room. Tuesday open mic still happens, just underground. The reviews are written in the register of Andrew Karpan and Aaron Tomey’s Grime Square: observational, footnoted, slightly cruel without being mean. The project owes them stylistically.

on hearing them

The ultrasonic vocalizations played in this piece are real recordings. Rats vocalize mostly above the human hearing range. The laboratory recordings from Tachibana’s open dataset were slowed about elevenfold, carrying their ultrasonic calls down into our register; the Freesound recordings are squeaks rats make within our hearing, played as recorded (see Credits). Leibniz called the sounds we don’t consciously perceive les petites perceptions. This is one way of moving them across the threshold.

The Yelp pastiche works in the other direction. The rats are granted a reviewer ID, a vocabulary, a star rating, an audience. All things they are normally denied. The piece is interested in both crossings.

on playback

Rats don’t write things down. Their speech is oral, never twice the same. Each playback you hear is a new performance of the same review, read in a different order of vocalizations, with different sample choices and timing. If you want to return to a specific playback (to share or study), there’s a toggle in the footer to switch from ‘in the moment’ to ‘on record’.

how to listen

  • Headphones strongly recommended.
  • Click any pin on the map to open a review.
  • Reviews accumulate; previously-heard rats keep talking quietly in the background.
